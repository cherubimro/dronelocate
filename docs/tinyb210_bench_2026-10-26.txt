WHAT IT MEANS
  1. TDOA IS VIABLE on these four boards: every pair agrees to 12-33 ns
     (std), 9-35 ns (MAD), and 4-25 ns within any 30 s. That is 4-10 m of
     range per pair, before geometry.
  2. Board 2 (1974XX) is the noisiest: its pairs are 16-29 ns std against
     12-18 ns for the others (the 1>3 pair's 33 ns std is a few outlier
     captures; its MAD is 18). 1974XX is also the board whose oscillator
     got stuck at -15 ppm earlier today. Worth watching; not disqualifying.
  3. The boards have CONSTANT offsets between them, -46 to +60 ns (~32 m
     of range if left uncalibrated). The loops close to <= 9 ns, so these
     numbers are real and self-consistent.
  4. IMPORTANT: THE OFFSETS DO NOT SURVIVE A POWER-CYCLE. Before the
     replug, the same pair 1>2 measured +218 ns (two-board run); after it,
     -20 ns, nothing moved. So a one-time calibration per board is NOT
     enough: the offsets must be measured again after every power-up --
     at the field site that means a reference transmitter at a surveyed
     point, measured at the start of each session (or continuously).
  5. Give every board >= 3 minutes after power-up before measuring: the
     frequency pulls in within ~2 min, but board 4's phase took ~30 s
     longer.
  6. Captures 60-100, 180-220 and 280 had no usable frame on any pair:
     the hotspot's traffic paused. Expected with a phone as the source; it
     costs captures, not accuracy.

NEXT STEPS
  1. Confirm point 4: power-cycle all four again and re-run without moving
     anything. If the offsets change again, per-session calibration is a
     hard requirement for the field.
  2. A longer run (30+ min) to see whether the offsets hold within a
     session and how large the slow wander is.
  3. Software: a per-node clock offset in the site config + supernode, and
     a calibration procedure that measures it (this tool already produces
     the numbers relative to one board).

EARLIER TODAY
  - Two-board run (1970XX vs 1974XX, before the replug): scatter 39.9 ns

  - Fixed today in the driver: a board could align exactly 1 s off
    (gps_time label); the host clock now decides the second.
  - 1974XX once ran free at -15.3 ppm (not disciplined) until power-cycled.

Part 2: RECEIVE SIDE -- GAIN, PORTS, ANTENNAS (evening, at home)
=====================================================================

BOARDS AND SETUP
  Boards 1 (1970XX) and 4 (1971XX), two 2.4 GHz antennas each, 6.1 cm
  apart, on the RX connectors. Desk router TP-Link_7D69 (channel 1) on for
  part of the tests; phone hotspot ESP32-Dioda used as a test source.

1. THE GAIN WAS TOO LOW FOR EVERY EARLIER RECEIVE TEST
   Noise floor (median power, dBFS) against receive gain:
                     0 dB    20 dB    40 dB    60 dB    76 dB
     board 1        -65.7    -61.9    -60.2    -44.1    -28.9
     board 4        -65.6    -61.9    -60.8    -45.5    -30.7
   Up to ~40 dB the floor barely moves: it is the converter's own noise.
   Above ~45 dB it rises ~0.9 dB per dB: the front end's real noise.
   -> At 30-40 dB (used all day) weak signals sit 15-20 dB under a masking
      floor: antennas, ports and directions all look the same.
   -> USE ~60 dB. The project's B210 site files and tools now default to 60
      (they were 32-40). At 60 a router ~1 m away peaked at 67% of full
      scale, so there is headroom.
   The receive chain itself works: both local oscillators lock
   (lo_locked true), tuned exactly to the requested frequency.

2. RX2 vs TX/RX MADE NO MEASURABLE DIFFERENCE ON THESE BOARDS
   At 60 dB, on every band from 800 MHz to 2.66 GHz (mobile networks and
   the router), selecting "RX2" or "TX/RX" in UHD gave identical power to
   0.0-0.1 dB on both boards. The ports are u.FL on the board, brought out
   by IPEX-to-SMA cables. Why the selection changes nothing is not
   established (how the clone wires the RF switch is one possibility); what
   it means for testing is that comparing "antenna port" with "empty TX/RX
   port" told us nothing today.

3. THE ANTENNAS WORK (the decisive test)
   Phone hotspot in ANOTHER ROOM (5-10 m, through a wall), channel 11,
   laptop Wi-Fi off, gain 60, 20 s per board, bursts > 6 dB counted:
                         antennas ON    antennas OFF
     board 4 (vertical)      408             4
     board 1 (horizontal)     49             6
   With antennas the distant phone is detected ~100x (board 4) / ~8x
   (board 1) more often: without them it is mostly below the threshold.
   Compare COUNTS, not the mean level of detected bursts: with antennas off
   only the rare strongest bursts pass the threshold, so their mean level
   looks higher and the comparison inverts (the script printed "-2 dB").

4. POLARISATION: MOUNT ANTENNAS VERTICAL
   Same phone, same room: board 4 with vertical antennas 408 detections,
   board 1 with horizontal antennas 49. Wi-Fi and drone links are mostly
   vertically polarised; a horizontal element also has a null along its
   own axis. Every field node: vertical antennas, all oriented alike.

5. A NEAR SOURCE IS NOT A TEST
   The router ~1 m away, per burst, gain 60: +18 to +22 dB with the
   antennas OFF, and the antennas added only 1-4 dB. At that range it
   couples into the cables and the board directly. The same effect made
   all of today's earlier tests near the router look port-independent.

6. BEARING (two-antenna direction finding) -- see Part 3: inconclusive
   (both channels responded to both antennas in that setup; cause open).

WHAT WENT WRONG IN MY TESTING TODAY (so it is not repeated)
  - I measured at 30-40 dB gain, where the converter's noise hides weak
    signals, and concluded the antennas did nothing. They work.
  - I compared "RX2" with "TX/RX" as antenna vs empty port; on this board
    the selection changed nothing, so the comparison was empty.
  - I relied on one sequential, burst-dependent comparison in the morning
    ("+24 dB vs +2.9 dB") and called the antennas verified; it was not a
    valid test either way.

CHANGES MADE TO THE PROJECT
  - config/site-b210.json, site-field.json, site-mixed-b210.json:
    B210 nodes and hardware.gain_db 32/36/40 -> 60 (with the reason).
  - node.py UHD fallback gain 40 -> 60; hw_selftest.py and
    tools/two_board_timing.py default gain -> 60.
  - docs/splitter_calibration.md: port section rewritten (RX2 vs TX/RX
    made no difference, physical antenna test at 60 dB with a far source,
    vertical mounting).
  - Project notes (both copies): priority item 1 updated with the four-board
    timing results, per-power-up offsets, GPSDO hangs, gain and antennas.
  - Driver: a board aligning one second off is now relabelled against the
    host clock; tools/two_board_timing.py handles N boards (all pairs, per-
    board offsets, loop closure) and opens boards one at a time.

NEXT

  2. Repeat the bearing test at 60 dB with vertical antennas.

Part 3: TWO-ANTENNA DIRECTION FINDING (night 26/27, board 4, at home)
=====================================================================

SETUP
  Board 4 (1971XX), two vertical 2.4 GHz antennas 6.1 cm apart (lambda/2)
  on its two RX connectors, gain 60, router off, laptop Wi-Fi off during
  each recording, phone hotspot as the source. Scripts wait for ENTER and
  count down 10 s before each 20 s recording.

1. BEARING: THE PHASE DOES NOT MOVE WITH DIRECTION
   Phone straight in front (A), 45 deg left (L), 45 deg right (R):
     < 1 m,   USB 3:  relative phase  0 / +3.5 / +16.7 deg
     3-5 m,   USB 3:                  0 / +3.2 /  +1.1 deg
     3-5 m,   USB 2:                  0 / +11.9 / +2.9 deg
   Expected about 0 / +-127 / -+127 deg. Coherence between the channels
   0.89-0.999 throughout: both channels see practically the same signal.

2. USB 3 RADIATES INTO BOTH RECEIVERS
   Coherence between channel 0 and 1 on NOISE ONLY (no bursts):
                   1000 MHz   2422 MHz   3500 MHz
     USB 3           0.50       0.47       0.54
     USB 2           0.03       0.01       0.08
   Two separate receivers have independent noise (~0). On USB 3 about half
   of the noise floor was a common signal: the board's own 5 Gbps link.
   Forced to USB 2 without a cable (as root):
     echo 1 > /sys/bus/usb/devices/usb2/2-0:1.0/usb2-port1/disable
   (undo with 0 and replug). On USB 2 the B210 needs
   recv_frame_size=2048,num_recv_frames=64 and <= 2 Msps x 2 channels, or
   libusb throws LIBUSB_TRANSFER_OVERFLOW intermittently.
   -> Field: ferrite or shielded USB 3 cables, antennas away from the
      cable, metal enclosure per node.

3. BOTH CHANNELS RECEIVE BOTH ANTENNAS (USB 2, phone 3-5 m, still)
   Strongest 10 % of bursts, above each channel's own floor:
                         channel 0     channel 1
     both antennas on     +25.3 dB      +25.1 dB
     left antenna off     +19.6 (-5.7)  +18.6 (-6.5)
     right antenna off    +22.0 (-3.3)  +20.5 (-4.6)
   Removing EITHER antenna lowers BOTH channels by about the same amount.
   With isolated inputs it would lower only its own channel. The receiver
   noise is independent (point 2), so the two receive chains are separate;
   where the coupling happens is NOT established. A shared RF path on the
   board is one explanation; mutual coupling between two antennas 6 cm
   apart, leakage between the u.FL cables and connectors, and a source
   close enough to couple into the cables directly (point 5 of Part 2) are
   others that this setup cannot rule out. Whatever the cause, the phase
   difference carried no direction here.

CONCLUSION
  - Two-antenna direction finding did not work in this setup, and the test
    does not say why. Do not conclude the board has one RX input: the B210
    design has two independent receive chains and the noise measurement
    agrees. Before blaming the board, repeat with the antennas isolated
    (separate cables, physical separation or a shield between them, a far
    source) and, if the coupling persists, ask HamGeek how RX1 and RX2 are
    wired. (Observed: removing either antenna lowers both channels by
    3-6 dB; channel coherence 0.999 on the same signal.)
  - TDOA is unaffected: it uses one channel per node.
  - USB 3 interference is real and large; mitigate it at every node.

Part 4: field, 2026-10-28 -- the field site, four TinyB210 nodes + HALO
=====================================================================

Source: the field session's commits (3b33c4d, 124739a, b4e7c63). The
field logs, the site file actually used and the per-pair measurement
files stayed on the field machines and are not in the repository.

1. THE DEVICE CLOCK DRIFTS AGAINST GPS
   The GPSTCXO steers the oscillator's frequency only roughly, and nothing
   holds the device clock's phase: it is set once, on a PPS edge, at
   alignment, and then walks away from GPS time.
     drift per board        about -1 ns/s (-550..-600 ns in 10 min)
     between boards         12-15 ns/min
     TDOA offsets in field  hundreds of ns, up to 2 us, growing with uptime
   This is what Part 1 read as "constant offsets that change at every
   power-up": its runs were a few minutes long, too short to tell drift
   from an offset.

2. THE CORRECTION (dronelocate/uhd_source.py)
   Every PPS edge is timestamped by the device. A thread polls
   get_time_last_pps at 5 Hz and records each edge's phase; a straight
   line through the last 60 edges gives the clock error at any moment,
   and every capture timestamp has it subtracted (metadata pps_corr_ns).
   One edge reads only to the tick (25 ns at 40 MHz); the fit through 60
   is well under that. Node health: pps_phase_ns, pps_phase_fit_ns,
   pps_drift_ns_per_s.

3. RESULT WITH THE CORRECTION
     all four nodes right after a full restart   within +-60 ns
     change over 15 min                          20-45 ns
     solver residual                             366 ns -> 10-28 ns

4. WHAT REMAINS
   A per-node offset of tens of ns. nodes[].timing_offset_ns in the site
   file takes it out; DL_MEAS_LOG=file.jsonl makes the supernode append
   every all-pairs burst's per-pair lags, which is the input a reference
   emitter at a known spot needs to measure those offsets. The reference
   emitter is still needed -- for tens of ns now, not microseconds.

5. RADAR (HALO) IN THE FIELD
   - DHCP moved it .231 -> .249 -> .237. radars[].serial now finds it at
     whatever address it answers on and ignores a second radar on the
     LAN; radars[].ip only picks the interface to discover on.
   - It powers up in standby. radars[].auto_transmit sends transmit on
     every connect, for an unattended site after a power cut. Opt-in in
     the site file, because the radar is a transmitter.

6. CONSOLE ON A SMALL SITE
   The nodes were ~23 m apart, less than one 34 m node marker. The console
   now scales markers, grid and camera to the node spread (vertical x1
   under 1 km), hides an uncertainty ellipsoid more than 20x the array
   behind an "unconstrained" banner, and shows the fleet status before
   the first fix.
